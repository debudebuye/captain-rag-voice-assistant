# Communication Protocol (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## VHF Radio Usage

The international general calling and distress channel is VHF Channel 16 (156.800 MHz).
Initial contact between vessels is made on Channel 16 and then switched to a working
channel for the actual conversation. The dedicated ship-to-ship working channel is VHF
Channel 6. VHF frequencies between 156.025 and 157.425 MHz are used for port operations.
When calling another vessel, give the vessel's name and call sign and the initiating
vessel's name in plain language. Belgium, the Netherlands, and the Baltic straits operate
restricted channels where Channel 16 must not be used for anything other than distress and
safety calls.

## Distress, Urgency, and Safety Calls

A distress call is prefixed with the word "MAYDAY" and is used when the vessel or crew are
in grave and imminent danger requiring immediate assistance. An urgency call is prefixed
with "PAN PAN" and is used when there is an urgent safety message concerning a vessel,
person, or cargo, but no immediate danger. A safety call is prefixed with "SECURITE" and is
used for navigational warnings and weather reports. The distress message is transmitted on
Channel 16 and repeated three times. After the call, listen for two seconds before
speaking again.

## Routine Traffic

Routine port entry and departure calls are made on the VTS (Vessel Traffic Service)
working channel in force for the port. When anchoring, position, depth, and the number of
shackles of cable paid out are reported to the VTS. All communications on the bridge are
recorded on the VDR, the voyage data recorder. Radio messages of a maritime-safety nature
are logged in the GMDSS logbook with the time and the channel used.

## GMDSS and Written Messages

The Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS) provides a framework for distress
alerting in coastal waters. Vessels must maintain a continuous watch on VHF Channel 70 for
Digital Selective Calling (DSC). A DSC distress alert, which transmits the vessel's
identity and position automatically, must be followed by a voice distress call on Channel
16. Written messages at sea use the IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases. Internal
communications between the bridge and the engine room are conducted on the internal phone
system and the telegraph, and every telegraph order is acknowledged back by the
engine-room by repetition.
