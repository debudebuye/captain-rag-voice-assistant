# Fire Procedure (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Fire Classes and Extinguishers

Fires on board are classified by type. Class A fires involve combustible solids such as
wood, paper, and textiles and are extinguished with water or foam. Class B fires involve
flammable liquids such as fuel oil and lubricating oil and are extinguished with foam,
dry chemical, or carbon dioxide. Class C fires involve gases and require dry powder.
Class D fires involve combustible metals and require special dry powder. Electrical
equipment fires, a special case often treated as Class C, must never be fought with water
and are extinguished with carbon dioxide or dry chemical.

## Fire Response on Board

When a fire is discovered, the person who finds it must shout "FIRE, FIRE, FIRE" and sound
the alarm immediately. The fire party assembles at the designated fire station and goes to
the scene wearing fire-fighting equipment and breathing apparatus. The fire must be
contained by closing watertight doors, shutting off the ventilation to the compartment, and
closing fuel supply valves. The sprinkler system should be activated if the fire is in an
accommodation space. For a fire in the engine room, the fixed CO2 system may be released
only after the Master has confirmed that all crew have been accounted for and have left the
engine room, because CO2 displaces oxygen and is lethal to any person inside.

## Engine Room Fire

An engine-room fire is one of the most dangerous situations on a ship. The Chief Engineer
stays in command of fighting the fire in the engine room until the order to release CO2 is
given, at which point everyone leaves the space. Ladders and escape routes must be kept
clear at all times. After the fire is extinguished, ventilation must be carried out before
any person is allowed to re-enter, and a re-entry check using a gas detector is mandatory.

## Fire in Accommodation Spaces

A fire in the accommodation requires the fire party to advance using a fire hose with a
branch, and all crew members who are not on the fire party are mustered at their lifeboat
stations. The sprinkler system, if fitted, may activate automatically on the compartment
where the heat detector activates. Ventilation must be shut down immediately and the
galley shutters closed. Corridor doors are kept closed. Do not open any door without
feeling the surface first for heat. If the door is hot, use the fire hose to cool the
door from a safe distance before opening it.

## Firefighting Equipment Inventory

Every vessel is required to carry specific fire-fighting equipment based on tonnage and
vessel type. The minimum inventory includes: portable extinguishers at every station on
the ship, including a CO2 extinguisher in each accommodation space and the engine room,
foam or dry chemical extinguishers for oil fires, and a fire blanket on the galley stove.
The fixed firefighting system for the engine room is usually a high-pressure CO2 flooding
system, which is operated from a room adjacent to the engine room containing the CO2
bottles and the control valves.
