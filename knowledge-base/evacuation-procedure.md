# Evacuation Procedure (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Abandon Ship Decision

The decision to abandon the ship rests solely with the Master. It is taken only when all
other options for saving the vessel and the crew have been exhausted and the vessel is
sinking, capsizing, or on fire beyond control. The Master broadcasts the abandon-ship alarm
and orders crew to the muster stations with their immersion suits and lifejackets, plus the
emergency provisions carried in their survival kit. The abandonment is supervised by the
Master and must not look like panic. Each lifeboat has a designated crew and a trained
lifeboat officer.

## Boarding and Lowering

Lifeboats are boarded by all crew members in an orderly manner. The second engineer and
the boat's coxswain board first to start the engine and free the davit brakes. The outer
lifeboat is boarded from the deck and the inner lifeboat is boarded from inside the ship
where the deck is lower. The lifeboat is lowered with the falls kept taut so that the boat
does not swing into the ship's side. Liferafts are thrown overboard and inflated using the
hydrostatic release unit when the rail is below the waterline. A 25-meter painter is
attached before the raft inflates.

## Man Overboard in Evacuation

During evacuation, if a person falls into the water, the lifeboat is manoeuvred to the
person with the sea on the lee side, and a throwing line is used. A rescue sling is never
to be thrown directly at the person; it is thrown beyond them and pulled back so that it
drops over them. Swimmers are assisted on board by rolling them over the gunwale at the
stern of the raft. Once aboard the raft, the sea anchor is deployed and the inflatable
canopy should be closed to keep the heat in and the spray out.

## Survival at Sea in a Raft

Survival in a liferaft follows a sequence: on entering the raft, immediately cut the
painter, deploy the sea anchor, and close the canopy. Check for personnel who are wounded
or cold and treat them first. The number of survivors is reported to the Master by the
boat's officer. Food and fresh water rations are rationed: about 1 liter of water per
person per day, and only on the Master's authorisation is this exceeded. Do not drink
seawater, and do not eat until the first 24 hours have passed. Fishing equipment from the
survival pack may be used. The raft should remain in position, with the sea anchor set,
until other vessels arrive or for as long as the seaworthiness of the raft allows.

## Abandon Ship Equipment Provisions

Lifeboats are equipped with provisions for 7 days: for each person, 3 liters of fresh
water, 10,000 kilojoules of food, one thermal protection aid, and a basic first-aid kit.
The survival pack includes a waterproof torch, signal rockets, a radio beacon, a radar
reflector, and a combined bailer-bucket. A seawater-activated battery operates the
emergency light inside the boat. The lifeboat's engine uses a petrol motor and is tested
for one minute by lowering the boat to the embarkation deck at least once per month.
