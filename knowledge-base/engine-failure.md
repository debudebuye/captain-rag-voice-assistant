# Engine Failure Procedure (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Immediate Actions

When a main engine failure occurs, the officer of the watch must first inform the Master
and the engine-room. The engine is to be slowed or stopped according to the situation, and
the propeller is to be stopped as soon as practical. The officer on watch must immediately
fix the ship's position by GPS, radar, or visual bearings and monitor the vessel's drift.
If the vessel is in a traffic lane, anchorage, or a narrow channel, the crew must display
the appropriate signals: two black balls by day or two all-round red lights by night to
indicate a vessel not under command.

## Steering and Headway Control

If the engine fails while coastal or in open sea, the vessel should be given the option to
drift while the engineers assess the fault. Mainshaft lubrication must be maintained using
the turning gear when the engine is stationary for more than 15 minutes. If the vessel has
bow thrusters, these can be used to keep head up to sea when drifting in heavy weather.
Anchor should be deployed only in an emergency and when depth and sea room permit.

## Engineering Response

The Chief Engineer organises the investigation. The engine room must check the governor
setting, fuel supply, and the cooling water system as the first steps. If the failure is
caused by fuel starvation, switch to the emergency fuel tank. For minor faults that can be
repaired within 30 minutes, the Master may decide to wait and repair at sea. If the repair
time is expected to be more than two hours, the Master should request tug assistance or
towing. A full report of the failure is entered in the engine-room log.

## Blackout Condition

A total blackout occurs when the main and auxiliary engines stop and all electrical power
is lost. The emergency generator must start automatically within 45 seconds and supply
power to the emergency switchboard, which feeds the navigation lights, the steering gear,
the emergency lighting, the fire pumps, and the communication equipment. Until the
emergency power is restored, the bridge team uses battery-operated torches and the UHF
radios are powered by recharging in the emergency generator room. The emergency generator
room's battery bank is tested weekly. After a blackout, the synchronising process is
started by the duty engineer and the main switchboard is re-energised before the main
engine can be restarted.
