# First Aid on Board (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Initial Assessment

When a casualty is found, assess the scene for safety before approaching. Check the
casualty for a response by shouting and gently shaking the shoulders. If unresponsive but
breathing, place the casualty in the recovery position on their side and monitor the
airway. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin CPR immediately. The chest
compressions are performed at a rate of 100 to 120 per minute, with a compression depth of
at least 5 centimeters in an adult, allowing the chest to recoil fully between
compressions. If a rescuer is alone, the ratio is 30 compressions to 2 rescue breaths.

## Automated External Defibrillator

The Automated External Defibrillator (AED) is carried on board and is stored on the bridge.
When a shockable rhythm is detected by the AED, make sure nobody is touching the casualty
and press the shock button when advised. Resume CPR immediately after the shock, starting
with compressions. The AED should be used as soon as it is available and must not delay
the start of chest compressions. Defibrillation is effective in the first few minutes of a
cardiac arrest and the chance of survival decreases by about 10 percent for every minute
of delay.

## Burns, Bleeding, and Hypothermia

For thermal burns, cool the burn with cool running water for at least 20 minutes and do
not apply creams or ice directly to the wound. Cover the burn with a sterile non-stick
dressing. For severe bleeding, apply firm direct pressure with a clean dressing; a
tourniquet is used only when direct pressure fails. For hypothermia, remove wet clothing,
warm the casualty gradually, and give warm drinks only if the person is fully conscious.
Never rub the skin of a hypothermic casualty. All first-aid cases are recorded in the
medical logbook by the designated medical officer.

## Medication and Medical Stores

The medical chest is sealed with a checklist and restocked after every trip. Medication is
stored in a locked cabinet in the sickbay, with the key held by the designated medical
officer. Common stock includes painkillers, anti-motion sickness tablets, rehydration
salts, and antacids. Radio medical advice can be requested from a coastal radio station
and is free of charge. A medical officer on shore can advise on prescriptions and further
transport of a seriously injured person; in such a case the vessel may divert to the
nearest port with a hospital. The IMO Medical First Aid Guide is kept on the bridge and
used as the reference for all treatment decisions.

## Prevention of Illness on Board

To prevent disease, the galley follows a strict hygiene checklist. Drinking water tanks are
chlorinated and tested weekly. Crew members handling food are required to wash their hands
and to wear gloves. Seafarers with contagious conditions are isolated in a single cabin
and every surface the sick person touched is disinfected. Garbage is stored in sealed bags
in the galley and disposed of in the port reception facilities or incinerated at sea only
where regulation allows.
