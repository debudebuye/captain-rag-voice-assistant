# Man Overboard (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Initial Response

When a person is seen to fall overboard, the lookout must shout "MAN OVERBOARD" and throw
the nearest lifebuoy to the person immediately. The bridge is notified at once and the
engine is stopped. The vessel is turned back to the person using a Williamson turn so that
the ship returns to its own wake and the man is brought on the lee side. The officer of the
watch sounds the man-overboard signal: three long blasts on the whistle. The time of the
incident and the position are recorded immediately, and the recovery team stands by at the
poop deck with a lifebuoy, a rescue line, and the rescue boat ready for lowering.

## Williamson Turn Steps

The Williamson turn is the standard recovery manoeuvre. First, the wheel is put hard over
to the side towards which the man has fallen. After the ship has deviated about 60 degrees
from its original heading, the wheel is reversed to the opposite side. When the ship is on
the reciprocal course to the original heading, the rudder is centred. This manoeuvre
brings the ship back along the original track so that the person is found on the ship's
lee side, directly abeam.

## Night and Adverse Conditions

At night or in heavy weather, recovery is more difficult. The search must use a systematic
expanding-square search pattern and the person in the water can be positively identified
using a marker light on the lifebuoy. The deck is to be heavily lit with all available
floodlights directed towards the water. A second person should be designated to keep the
man in sight at all times, pointing at him continuously. The rescue boat is only launched
when sea state and training permit; otherwise the main recovery is by hook and line.

## Person in Water Rescue

For a person in the water wearing a lifejacket, the recovery team approaches downwind so
that the casualty is brought to the lee side of the boat. A crew member wearing a safety
line and a lifejacket is lowered over the side on the pilot ladder to assist the casualty.
The casualty is lifted using a Jacob's ladder and the recovery sling, taking care not to
further injure the person. Hypothermia is the main risk after a person has been in the
water: the casualty is assessed for breathing and consciousness, then treated according
to the hypothermia procedure, which includes removing wet clothing and warming the person
gradually.

## Prevention Measures

Prevention is the most effective control. A permit is required before any person is
allowed to work over the side. Safety lines must be rigged whenever crew work on the open
deck in adverse weather, and personal flotation devices are mandatory for anyone on deck
at night. Lifelines are rigged from the accommodation entrance to the bow. Persons working
over the side on stages wear a safety harness attached to a strong point, and a second
person supervises them at all times.
