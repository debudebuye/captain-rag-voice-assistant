# Emergency Procedures (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Emergency Command Structure

On any ship, the highest-ranking officer on the bridge is the On-Scene Commander. In an
emergency this is normally the Master, or the Chief Officer if the Master is incapacitated.
The Commander's primary duty is to protect life, then the environment, then property. All
crew members must be able to identify the On-Scene Commander and receive orders only from
authorised officers. If an emergency occurs, the alarm bell is sounded with seven short
blasts followed by one long blast. Every crew member who hears the general alarm must go to
their muster station immediately, wearing a lifejacket and warm clothing.

## Emergency Response Steps

The general response to any emergency follows a fixed sequence: Sound the alarm, identify
the nature and location of the danger, must the crew at their stations, establish
communications with the bridge, and then execute the specific procedure for the situation.
The Chief Engineer and deck officers confirm to the bridge that the crew are present using
the muster list. All actions are recorded in the deck log and the engine-room log by the
officer of the watch.

## Debriefing and Reporting

After an emergency is resolved, the Master must prepare a detailed report. The report must
include the time of the incident, the nature of the emergency, the actions taken, and any
injuries or damage. A verbal report to the company office must be made within one hour of
the incident. Written reports are submitted within 24 hours. Drill schedules require that
at least one emergency drill is carried out every month, and lifeboat drills must be held
within 24 hours of leaving port.

## Alarm Signals and Muster List

Every crew member is issued with an individual muster card which states the alarm signal
for each emergency type and the duties on muster. The emergency signals are: general
emergency, seven short blasts and one long blast on the whistle and on the general alarm
bells; fire, continuous ringing of the alarm bells; and man overboard, three long blasts.
The fire drill and the abandon-ship drill are combined in one monthly drill. Muster
stations are marked with a green arrow and are located at the boat deck. The muster list
is posted on the bridge, in the alleyways, and near the accommodation entrances.

## Security Levels

Security levels are set by the port authority and communicated to the ship. Security Level
1 is the normal operating level. At Security Level 2, extra measures apply, including
restricting access to the engine room and logging every visitor on board. At Security
Level 3, the highest level, ship security operations are active and the Master coordinates
with the port's response team; the vessel may be asked to move to a designated anchorage
and personnel access is limited to security-cleared persons only.
