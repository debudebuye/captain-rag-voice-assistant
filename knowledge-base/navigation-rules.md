# Navigation Rules (SAMPLE — demo knowledge base)

> This document is sample content created for a demo knowledge base. It is not
> official maritime guidance and must not be used for real ship operations.

## Collision Avoidance

Every vessel must maintain a proper lookout by sight and hearing as well as by all
available means appropriate in the prevailing conditions. A power-driven vessel under way
shall keep out of the way of sailing vessels, vessels engaged in fishing, and vessels not
under command or restricted in their ability to manoeuvre. When two power-driven vessels
meet head-on so as to involve risk of collision, each shall alter course to starboard so
that each passes on the port side of the other. When two power-driven vessels are crossing,
the vessel which has the other on its starboard side shall give way and shall, if the
circumstances of the case admit, avoid crossing ahead of the other vessel.

## Lights and Shapes

Between sunset and sunrise, or in restricted visibility, a power-driven vessel under way
shall exhibit a masthead light forward, a second masthead light aft and higher than the
forward one, sidelights, and a stern light. A vessel at anchor shall exhibit an all-round
white light in the fore part. A vessel over 100 meters in length is also required to use
her working lights to illuminate her decks.

## Restricted Visibility

In or near an area of restricted visibility, whether by day or night, vessels shall go at a
safe speed adapted to the prevailing circumstances. Every vessel shall have engines ready
for immediate manoeuvre. A power-driven vessel detecting by radar alone the presence of
another vessel shall determine whether a close-quarters situation is developing or risk of
collision exists. If so, she shall take avoiding action in ample time and avoid an
alteration of course to port for a vessel forward of her beam.

## Navigation in Coastal and Narrow Waters

In narrow channels, a power-driven vessel under 20 meters in length, or less than 100
meters, shall not impede the passage of a vessel that can safely navigate only within the
channel. Vessels crossing a channel are required to avoid impeding a vessel following the
channel. Every vessel navigating in or near a traffic separation scheme must respect the
direction of traffic flow in the separation lane. The rule of the road for overtaking:
prepare to meet boats overtaking on the starboard side. The vessel being overtaken has the
right of way and must hold its course and speed until overtaken.

## Sound Signals

The whistle signals are used to communicate intentions: one short blast means "I am
altering my course to starboard"; two short blasts mean "I am altering my course to port";
three short blasts mean "I am operating astern propulsion". When vessels are in sight of
one another and questions arise, a minimum of five short and rapid blasts on the whistle
signals doubt or disagreement with the other vessel's actions, which requires the stand-on
vessel to pay attention to the give-way vessel's intentions.
